How Much Can I Borrow on an $80k Salary in Australia?

Updated 2 September 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer: there is no reliable lender-wide borrowing figure for an $80,000 salary. Your result can change materially with household expenses, dependants, credit limits, other debts, loan type and the lender's current assessment settings. Use a calculator for a transparent scenario, then confirm it with a lender or broker.

What changes the result

  • Income after tax: a lender may treat overtime, bonuses, allowances and a second job differently from base income.
  • Household costs: lenders use their own expense benchmarks and can use the higher of your declared costs or their internal assessment. BorrowPower uses a transparent expense floor, not a lender's HEM figure.
  • Existing commitments: car finance, personal loans, BNPL and even unused credit-card limits can reduce serviceability.
  • Assessment rate: lenders test whether repayments remain affordable above the product rate. APRA's mortgage-lending standard requires a 3 percentage-point serviceability buffer for ADIs.

How to test an $80k scenario

Start with your base salary and actual regular expenses. Add every recurring debt payment and the full limit of each credit card. If you expect a change such as a child, a car loan or a reduced work week, run that as a separate scenario rather than assuming today's result will hold.

  1. Run a baseline with your current expenses and no optimistic income assumptions.
  2. Run a cautious version with higher household costs or a future debt payment.
  3. Use the repayment estimator to check whether a loan amount still feels manageable at a higher rate.

Before you rely on an estimate

  • Keep the purchase deposit separate from duty, conveyancing and other buying costs.
  • Reduce or close unused credit limits only where that makes sense for you.
  • Ask a lender how it treats your income type, debts and household expenses.
  • Do not treat an online result as a pre-approval or a property budget on its own.
